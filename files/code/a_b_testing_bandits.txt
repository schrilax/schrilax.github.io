In machine learning and data science, statistical decision-making is fundamental for evaluating and
optimizing different strategies. Two popular techniques used for this purpose are:

• A/B Testing: A controlled experiment comparing two or more variants (A and B) to determine which
performs better.

• Multi-Armed Bandits (MAB): A reinforcement learning algorithm that balances exploration (trying
new options) and exploitation (capitalizing on known rewards).

Both approaches are widely used in online advertising, product optimization, and user experience testing.

A/B Testing

A/B Testing is a classical statistical method for comparing two or more groups by assigning participants
randomly to each group and measuring the effect of an intervention.

Key Steps in A/B Testing

1. Hypothesis Formulation:
• 𝐻0 : No difference between groups.
• 𝐻1 : A statistically significant difference exists.

2. Random Assignment: Users are randomly assigned to either group A or group B.

3. Outcome Measurement: The effectiveness of the variants is measured using metrics like conversion rate, click-through rate (CTR) or revenue.

4. Statistical Significance Testing: Techniques such as the t-test or chi-squared test are used to determine if
the difference is statistically significant.

In A/B testing, the p-value is the probability that the observed difference between two versions (A and B) is due to random chance alone, assuming there is no real difference between them. 

A low p-value (typically below 0.05) indicates that the results are statistically significant, suggesting the difference is likely not due to chance. 

A high p-value means the results could be random, and the experiment may need more data or to be re-run.

How to interpret the p-value

Low p-value (e.g., <0.05): The result is statistically significant. This means it is highly unlikely that the difference was caused by chance, and you can be confident that the change in version B is the reason for the difference.

High p-value (e.g., >0.05): The result is not statistically significant. The observed difference is likely due to random chance, and you cannot conclude that version B is better than version A.  Borderline p-value (e.g., between \(0.05\) and \(0.1\)): The results are not conclusive. You may need to run the test longer to gather more data.

Key things to remember

P-value is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true: It only tells you the probability of seeing your result if the null hypothesis (no difference) were true.

Define your significance level (alpha) beforehand: A common threshold (alpha) is 0.05, but this can vary. Your p-value should be compared to this pre-determined alpha level to determine significance.

Avoid peeking: Checking the results of your A/B test too frequently can artificially lower the p-value and lead to premature conclusions.

P-value and effect size: Don't just look at the p-value. Also consider the effect size (how big the difference is) and confidence intervals to get a more complete picture of your results.

Advantages of A/B Testing
• Simple to implement and interpret.
• Reliable when sample sizes are large.
• Provides statistically significant results.

Limitations of A/B Testing
• Inefficient for dynamic environments with continuous changes.
• Requires large sample sizes to detect small effects.
• Exploration-exploitation dilemma: Fixed allocations do not adapt to new information.

Multi-Armed Bandits

The Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) problem models the challenge of choosing between multiple competing options
with uncertain rewards.

Key Concepts
1. Arms: The different options or variants being tested (e.g., A, B, C, etc.).
2. Reward: The payoff or outcome associated with selecting an arm.
3. Exploration: Trying out different arms to gather information.
4. Exploitation: Choosing the arm with the highest expected reward.

Bandit Algorithms
1. Epsilon-Greedy:
• With probability ϵ, explore a random arm.
• With probability 1−ϵ, exploit the best-known arm.

2. Upper Confidence Bound (UCB): Selects the arm with the highest upper confidence bound, balancing
exploration and exploitation.

3. Thompson Sampling: Uses Bayesian inference to sample from the posterior distribution and select the arm
with the highest reward.

Advantages of Multi-Armed Bandits
• Efficiently balances exploration and exploitation.
• Adaptable to dynamic environments.
• Requires fewer samples than A/B testing.

Limitations of Multi-Armed Bandits
• More complex to implement.
• May suffer from delayed convergence.
• Requires careful parameter tuning.
